# Open Questions and Research Backlog

**Status:** Active discovery backlog. This is not an implementation backlog.

## Priority 0 — Resolve before MVP approval

1. Which caregiver segment experiences the strongest recurring bedtime problem?
2. Which initial age band can be served safely and well?
3. Is the strongest value personalised stories, a screen-free audio ritual, or professionally produced audio drama?
4. What story duration and audio style do caregivers prefer?
5. Which first platform best supports screen-off playback, offline use, and caregiver distribution?
6. Which launch jurisdiction and initial language(s) are practical?
7. What level of personalisation creates value without collecting sensitive or unnecessary child data?
8. What minimum narration, character consistency, mix, and content-quality thresholds are acceptable?
9. Which provider combination meets quality, safety, rights, privacy, reliability, and cost requirements?
10. What price and usage allowance are acceptable to caregivers?
11. What are the legal and store-policy obligations for the selected market and platform?
12. What is the maximum affordable cost per accepted story and active family?

## Priority 1 — Resolve before private beta

- What is the smallest useful parent onboarding flow?
- Should story creation happen on demand, in advance, or both?
- How should the app handle generation failure at bedtime?
- Which stories should be cached or available offline?
- What deletion and retention periods are expected and affordable?
- How should caregivers report a concern and receive a response?
- Which analytics are necessary without collecting unnecessary child data?
- How will language, pronunciation, cultural representation, and accessibility be reviewed?
- What support response time can the team sustain?
- What device and network matrix is required for confidence?

## Priority 2 — After validated MVP

- Multiple caregiver profiles and shared family libraries.
- Additional languages and regional storytelling traditions.
- Carefully reviewed seasonal story collections.
- Optional caregiver-created story prompts with bounded inputs.
- Partnerships with educators, librarians, authors, or audio professionals.
- Additional platform distribution, only if demand justifies it.

## Research tasks and outputs

| Task | Method | Output | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Caregiver problem interviews | 12–20 adult caregivers | Synthesised themes and evidence gaps | Product |
| Concept test | Compare two concept cards | Preference, objections, and switching intent | Product/UX |
| Audio benchmark | Same script across candidates | Scorecard and sample library | Audio/AI |
| End-to-end prototype | One short, reviewed story | Pipeline demo and failure log | Architecture/AI |
| Unit-cost model | Provider usage and delivery assumptions | Low/base/high cost scenarios | Product/Engineering |
| Privacy and policy review | Data-flow review and qualified counsel | Market-specific obligations register | Trust/Legal |
| Platform comparison | Device needs, APIs, store rules, effort | Decision record | Architecture |
| Launch-channel test | Landing page or caregiver waitlist | Qualified interest and conversion evidence | Growth |

## Research discipline

- Record date, source, method, sample, limitations, and confidence.
- Separate observed evidence from assumptions and recommendations.
- Do not treat competitor claims as proof of customer demand.
- Do not treat interview enthusiasm as willingness to pay.
- Do not collect identifying information from children for discovery.
- Revisit conclusions when contradictory evidence appears.
