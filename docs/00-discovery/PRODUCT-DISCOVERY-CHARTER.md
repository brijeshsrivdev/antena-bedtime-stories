# Product Discovery Charter

**Status:** Draft for review. No implementation is authorised until discovery is explicitly approved.

## Vision
Help children look forward to a calming, magical bedtime ritual through personalised stories delivered as rich audio experiences.

## Product objective
Create a trusted, child-safe bedtime storytelling companion combining original age-appropriate stories with expressive narration, consistent character voices, music, ambience, and sound effects, while helping families build a healthy bedtime routine.

## Users
- Primary listener: child, across distinct developmental age bands.
- Primary decision-maker: parent or caregiver, responsible for setup, safety, permissions, and payment.
- Secondary user: family member helping select or save stories.

## Core value proposition
A story feels made for this child tonight, sounds like a carefully produced audio drama, and remains easy to replay when it becomes a favourite.

## Product principles
1. Audio-first: stories should work with the screen off.
2. Calm by design: no streak guilt, endless feeds, or stimulating bedtime loops.
3. Child-safe by default: age-appropriate generation, privacy minimisation, parental controls, and reporting.
4. Parent-trustworthy: transparent AI use, predictable costs, understandable controls, and reliable playback.
5. Personal without requiring personal data: names are optional; do not request sensitive details.
6. Quality before quantity: coherence, emotional pacing, intelligibility, and sound balance matter more than generation speed.
7. Resilient playback: avoid audible gaps and network-dependent surprises.
8. Accessible and inclusive across languages, accents, cultures, family structures, and disabilities.
9. Evidence-led: separate verified facts, assumptions, and experiments.
10. Platform-aware: design mobile and tablet intentionally; confirm initial OS scope during discovery.

## Provisional experience
1. Parent sets age band, language, content boundaries, and bedtime preferences.
2. Child opens a calm home screen and chooses a theme or asks for a story.
3. Optional names and duration personalise the story.
4. The system plans a story, checks safety and coherence, then produces narration and a layered soundscape.
5. Audio is prepared or streamed without audible gaps.
6. The child can pause, resume, replay, favourite, or finish with a gentle ending.
7. Saved stories can be replayed without regeneration.

## Provisional non-goals for the first release
- Open-ended AI companionship or unrestricted voice chat.
- Social feeds, public child profiles, or child-to-child messaging.
- Advertising targeted at children.
- Gamification designed to maximise screen time.
- Video-first storytelling.
- Facial recognition, location tracking, or unnecessary sensitive-data collection.
- Unsubstantiated clinical sleep-benefit claims.

## Success measures to validate
- Parent-reported bedtime satisfaction and trust.
- Story completion, segmented by duration and age band.
- Playback failure and audible interruption rates.
- Voluntary story replay and favourite saves.
- Voluntary return on later nights, interpreted alongside parent feedback—not as an addiction target.
- Cost per completed story and listening hour.
- Safety incident rate and resolution time.
- Accessibility and comprehension feedback across target languages.

Targets must follow baseline research and usability testing; do not invent targets before evidence exists.

## Discovery questions
- Which age band and launch geography should come first?
- Which languages and accents are essential at launch?
- Which mobile/tablet operating systems are in scope?
- What story duration fits each age band?
- Should children speak to the app, choose options, or use parent-assisted controls?
- What personalisation is valuable without unnecessary data collection?
- Which audio capabilities meet quality, latency, safety, licensing, and cost requirements?
- What pricing do parents consider fair?
- What legal requirements apply in candidate launch markets?

## Approval gate
Discovery is complete only when the product objective, MVP scope, research evidence, requirements, UX, architecture, safety/privacy, cost model, roadmap, launch plan, and go-to-market plan have owners, reviews, recorded decisions, and explicit approval.
