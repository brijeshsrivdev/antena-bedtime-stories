# Caregiver Discovery Interview Guide

**Status:** Ready for research use with adults. Not a product validation claim.

## Research goal

Understand the real bedtime problem, existing routines, caregiver trust requirements, and willingness to try or pay for an audio-first story experience.

## Recruitment

- Recruit 12–20 adult parents or caregivers.
- Seek variety in child age, household routine, language, and current bedtime tools.
- Do not recruit children for this first round.
- Do not ask for children's names, photos, recordings, school, exact birth dates, or other identifying details.
- Explain the purpose, voluntary nature, note-taking, and retention plan before starting.

## 30-minute interview

### 1. Recent behaviour (10 minutes)

1. Think about the last time you put your child to bed. What happened from start to finish?
2. Which part was easiest? Which part was difficult?
3. What do you usually do when your child asks for another story or does not settle?
4. What do you currently use: books, your own storytelling, audio, video, music, or something else?
5. When did your current approach last fail? What did you do next?
6. How much time does bedtime usually take? Ask for a typical range, not an ideal answer.

### 2. Needs and trade-offs (7 minutes)

1. What would make bedtime feel successful for you?
2. Is the main need less effort for the caregiver, a calmer transition, a richer story, language support, or something else?
3. What makes a story feel comforting rather than overstimulating?
4. Would you prefer a short story that is always ready, or a personalised story that takes time to prepare?
5. What would make you stop using a bedtime app?

### 3. Concept comparison (8 minutes)

Present two neutral cards in random order:

- **Concept A — Personal story maker:** choose a theme and optional character details to create a new story.
- **Concept B — Bedtime audio companion:** listen to a carefully produced story with expressive narration, gentle music, ambience, and simple playback controls.

Ask:
1. Which would you try first, and why?
2. What feels unclear or untrustworthy?
3. Would you use it with the screen off?
4. How important is replaying a favourite story exactly as before?
5. What would you need to know about privacy, AI, voices, and content review?

Do not imply either concept already exists or is proven.

### 4. Pricing and adoption (5 minutes)

1. Have you paid for a children's audio, reading, or story product? What did you pay for?
2. What would make this worth paying for rather than using a free alternative?
3. Which feels more acceptable: a limited free allowance, a free trial, or a paid subscription?
4. What monthly price would feel so low you would question the quality? What feels reasonable? What feels too expensive?
5. Would you prefer a clear monthly story-generation limit with unlimited replay of saved stories?

Use open-ended questions before offering price ranges. Record actual past behaviour separately from hypothetical intent.

## Interviewer rules

- Ask for specific examples before asking opinions.
- Avoid leading questions such as “Wouldn't this be helpful?”
- Do not sell the product during the interview.
- Record contradictory and negative feedback.
- Do not interpret politeness as purchase intent.
- Store anonymised notes with a participant code. Keep consent records separately and only as needed.

## Synthesis template

For each interview, record:
- Participant code and broad context.
- Recent bedtime routine.
- Current workaround and its shortcomings.
- Trigger for seeking an alternative.
- Most important desired outcome.
- Trust, safety, and privacy concerns.
- Reaction to both concepts.
- Evidence of actual spending or switching.
- Strong quote, anonymised.
- Confidence and alternative interpretations.

## Decision criteria

After 12–20 interviews, summarise patterns and disconfirming evidence. Do not claim statistical significance or product-market fit. Decide whether to narrow the audience, refine the proposition, run a prototype test, or stop.
