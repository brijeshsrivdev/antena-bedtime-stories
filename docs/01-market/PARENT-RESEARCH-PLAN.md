# Parent/Caregiver Discovery Research Plan

## Objective
Test whether bedtime storytelling is a frequent, meaningful problem and whether parents value personalised audio drama enough to change existing habits or pay for a new product.

## Participants
Recruit 12–20 adult parents/caregivers as an initial qualitative sample across several household contexts and child age bands. This is directional research, not a statistically representative survey. Avoid collecting children's names, recordings, school details, or other identifying information. Do not interview children without a separate ethics/safeguarding plan and appropriate parent consent.

## Recruitment mix
- Families who read aloud.
- Families who already use audio stories, podcasts, smart speakers, or story apps.
- Families who use video at bedtime.
- Families who have tried and abandoned a bedtime app.
- Different child age bands, languages, accessibility needs, and household routines.

## Interview guide
1. Tell me about the last three bedtimes; what happened from start to sleep?
2. What is hardest about that routine, if anything?
3. What does your child ask for repeatedly?
4. Which tools or stories do you use today, and what do you pay?
5. What do you like and dislike about current options?
6. How do you feel about AI-generated stories? What would make you trust or reject them?
7. What would concern you about character names, microphones, child profiles, or saved history?
8. Would immersive sound effects/music improve the experience or become distracting?
9. Would you prefer curated stories, personalised stories, or both? Why?
10. What would make you choose this instead of reading aloud, YouTube, audiobooks, or a screen-free device?
11. What is the ideal story length and how should the experience end?
12. What would you expect to be free, and what would justify paying?
13. What would make you stop using it after a week?
14. May we contact you for a later prototype test? Store contact details separately from notes and only with consent.

## Concept test
Present two neutral concepts in random order:
- A personalised story generator with optional character names.
- A bedtime audio companion with expressive narration, character voices, atmosphere, music, and favourites.
Ask what the participant thinks each does, who it is for, what seems valuable, what feels risky, and which they would try first. Avoid leading language such as “magical” until after unaided reactions.

## Synthesis
Tag observations as behaviour, pain, workaround, desired outcome, objection, trust concern, or willingness-to-pay signal. Separate direct quotes from researcher interpretation. Record contradictory evidence and segments where the need is weak.

## Decision threshold
Do not claim product-market fit from this sample. Use findings to choose a narrow first segment, revise assumptions, and decide whether a larger quantitative test is warranted.
