# Private Beta and Launch Readiness

**Status:** Planning checklist. No beta or public launch is authorised.

## Beta purpose

Test whether caregivers find the experience useful, trustworthy, calm, and reliable. Beta is not a growth contest. It is a controlled learning phase with explicit stop conditions.

## Entry criteria

- Discovery gates have recorded approval.
- The selected platform and launch jurisdiction have been reviewed.
- Core P0 requirements and acceptance tests pass.
- Provider rights and data terms are documented.
- Safety validation cannot be bypassed in tested flows.
- Parent-facing privacy and AI disclosures are reviewed.
- Parent data deletion has been tested end to end.
- Generation quotas, rate limits, cost alerts, and incident contacts work.
- Support, reporting, rollback, and release ownership are assigned.
- A beta consent and feedback process is ready.

## Beta cohort

Start with a small group of adult caregivers recruited through consent-based channels. Avoid collecting child identifiers or recordings. Explain that this is a limited test, what data is collected, how to report concerns, and how to leave.

Do not expose children to experimental outputs that have not passed the product's required review and safety process.

## Observe

- Time and success rate from story request to playable audio.
- Generation rejection and retry rate.
- Playback starts, interruptions, and technical failures.
- Audio quality and content concerns reported by caregivers.
- Parent-reported usefulness and trust.
- Cost per accepted story and per active family.
- Deletion requests and completion.
- Confusing UX, billing, or privacy disclosures.

Collect only data needed for these questions. Define retention before collection.

## Stop conditions

Pause beta if any of these occur:
- A credible harmful-content incident.
- A privacy/security incident or cross-family data exposure.
- Unclear rights to generated or included audio.
- A bypass of pre-play validation.
- Unexpected spend beyond the approved cap.
- Repeated failures that make bedtime use unreliable.
- Caregivers misunderstand purchase, data, or deletion terms.

Assign an incident owner and a documented restart approval process.

## Launch readiness

- Store metadata, screenshots, age rating, and privacy disclosures match the actual product.
- All external SDKs and provider data flows are reviewed.
- Purchase and cancellation flows are tested if monetisation is enabled.
- Accessibility, supported devices, languages, and known limitations are published.
- Support inbox, response ownership, and safety escalation are operational.
- Monitoring and budget alerts are live.
- Backup, deletion, rollback, and incident playbooks are tested.
- Marketing claims are supported by evidence and do not promise therapeutic outcomes.
- A launch-day owner and post-launch review cadence are assigned.

## Post-launch review

At 7, 30, and 60 days, review quality, trust, cost, safety, acquisition, and support. These are proposed checkpoints; cadence should match the launch plan. Expand only when guardrails remain healthy.
